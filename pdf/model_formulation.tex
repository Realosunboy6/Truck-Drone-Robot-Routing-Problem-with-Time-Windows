\noindent This document writes out the mixed-integer linear program (MILP)
implemented in \texttt{model/capped\_flexible\_docking\_ordered\_sortie\_model.py},
restructured to follow the mathematical format of the VRP-DR paper
(Malik et al., ``Collaborative Last-Mile Delivery: A Multi-Platform Vehicle
Routing Problem With En-route Charging,'' arXiv:2505.23584) and the relaxed
penalized objective of the original truck--drone--robot formulation (Eq.~12 of
the TDRP paper). Every symbol, set, and constraint corresponds to named
variables, constraints, or helper blocks in the code (items marked
\emph{new} are specified here and land in the code with the phased migration
described below), so the formulation can be checked against the source
directly.

\paragraph{Implementation status.} The migration from the previous strict
single-trip model proceeds in phases: (1)~cyclic-sortie timing fix
(\texttt{dep\_t} ready times); (2)~relaxed objective with excess variables,
$\Gamma$ makespan, and $\alpha$ blend; (3)~energy-per-sortie filter;
(4)~trip-indexed multi-trip; (5)~full battery/charging variables. Until a phase
lands, the corresponding \emph{new} items are specification, not code.

\section{VRP-DR Feature Map}
\label{sec:features}

The five distinguishing features of VRP-DR are adopted as follows
($\checkmark$ = formulated in this document):

{\small
\begin{longtable}{@{}p{0.22\textwidth}p{0.60\textwidth}c@{}}
\toprule
VRP-DR feature & Where it lives in this formulation & \\
\midrule
Multi-visit service & Ordered sorties serve up to \texttt{MAX\_CUSTOMERS\_PER\_SORTIE} customers (Section~\ref{sec:sorties}) & $\checkmark$ \\
Multi-trip operations & Trip-indexed platforms $(d,q)$ with inter-trip sequencing (Sections~\ref{sec:variables}, \ref{sec:multitrip}) & $\checkmark$ \\
Flexible docking & Independent launch/recovery truck assignment $h^L \ne h^R$ (Section~\ref{sec:sync}) & $\checkmark$ \\
Cyclic and acyclic operations & Self-loop legs $(i,i)\in A^+$ plus truck ready times $\mathrm{dep}^t$ (Sections~\ref{sec:sorties}, \ref{sec:truck-time}) & $\checkmark$ \\
En-route charging & Per-sortie energy $e(s)$, battery $b_{p,q}$, charging $c_{p,q}$ during truck aboard time (Section~\ref{sec:charging}) & $\checkmark$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
}

\noindent In addition, the objective carries the VRP-DR cost--makespan blend
$\alpha\cdot\mathrm{OpCost} + (1-\alpha)\,\Gamma$
(Section~\ref{sec:objective}), and the hard capacity/endurance constraints of
the previous model are relaxed into the penalized form of the original TDRP
formulation's Eq.~12 (Sections~\ref{sec:rel-cap}, \ref{sec:rel-end}).

\section{Ordered Sortie Candidates}
\label{sec:sorties}

Before the MILP is built, \texttt{generate\_ordered\_sorties} enumerates, for
every leg $(i,k)\in A^+$ and every ordered subsequence of customers of length
$1,\dots,\texttt{MAX\_CUSTOMERS\_PER\_SORTIE}$, a candidate \emph{ordered sortie}
$s$ with:

\begin{itemize}
\item launch node $\iota(s) = i$, recovery node $\kappa(s) = k$ (with $i=k$
allowed: a \emph{cyclic} sortie);
\item ordered customer sequence $\sigma(s) = (\sigma_1(s),\dots,\sigma_{m(s)}(s))$, $\sigma_l(s)\in C$;
\item payload $q(s) = \sum_{c\,\in\,\sigma(s)} q_c$, kept only if $q(s) \le$ platform capacity (hard, faithful to Eq.~3 of the original formulation);
\item travel distance $\delta(s) = \sum_{(u,v)\,\in\,\mathrm{edges}(s)} \mathrm{dist}_{uv}$ (drone: \texttt{distance\_matrix}; robot: \texttt{robot\_distance\_matrix});
\item duration $\tau(s) = \sum_{(u,v)\,\in\,\mathrm{edges}(s)} \mathrm{time}_{uv}$;
\item cost $\gamma(s) = \sum_{(u,v)\,\in\,\mathrm{edges}(s)} \mathrm{cost}_{uv}$;
\item \textbf{energy} $e(s) = \big(a_p + b_p\,q(s)\big)\,\delta(s)$ in Wh, with platform energy coefficients $(a_p,b_p)$ (Section~\ref{sec:parameters}). Because the sequence and per-leg payloads are known at enumeration time, energy is precomputed exactly as VRP-DR's payload-dependent drone formula requires, but no energy nonlinearity ever enters the MILP.
\end{itemize}

\noindent where
\[
\mathrm{edges}(s) = \big((\iota(s),\sigma_1(s)),(\sigma_1(s),\sigma_2(s)),\dots,(\sigma_{m(s)}(s),\kappa(s))\big).
\]
For a cyclic leg $(i,i)$, $\mathrm{edges}(s)$ is the genuine cycle
$i \to \sigma_1 \to \cdots \to \sigma_m \to i$.
For each leg, only the \texttt{TOP\_SORTIES\_PER\_TRUCK\_LEG} candidates with
the most customers (ties broken by lowest cost, then duration) are kept.
$S^d$ uses drone time/distance matrices and $(Q_d, B_d)$; $S^r$ uses robot
time/distance matrices and $(Q_r, B_r)$.

\paragraph{Generation filters (relaxed model).} Because endurance violations are
now \emph{priced} rather than forbidden, generation keeps sorties with
$e(s) \le E_{\mathrm{slack}}\cdot B_p$ ($E_{\mathrm{slack}}\ge 1$, default $1.5$),
so the $\lambda_E$ penalties have candidates to price. The legacy distance caps
$E_d, E_r$ remain as documented non-binding sanity caps. Sorties with
$e(s) > B_p$ are still discarded only when $E_{\mathrm{slack}} = 1$.

Therefore, an ``integer optimal solution'' from the solver is optimal over the
retained capped candidate-sortie sets $S^d$ and $S^r$. It is not a proof of
global optimality over every possible ordered sortie in the unrestricted problem.

\paragraph{Penalty scaling.} Each $\lambda$ must exceed the maximum marginal
operating-cost saving from one unit of violation (exact-penalty theory) but not
by orders of magnitude: with $\lambda_T = 1000$ against a $\sim$\$68 solution,
$0.07$ units of violation swamp the entire objective (observed: route-duration
penalty reached $\sim$97\% of the objective before the per-instance $T_{\max}$
repair). Suggested starting values are in the table; calibrate per instance
family.

\subsection{Travel-cost coefficients}

\begin{align}
\mathrm{tcost}_{ij} &= \mathrm{tt}_{ij}\,C_w + \mathrm{dist}_{ij}\,C_{veh}, & (i,j)\in A \label{eq:tcost}\\
\mathrm{dcost}_{ij} &= \mathrm{dt}_{ij}\,C^d_w + \mathrm{dist}_{ij}\,C_{drone}, & i,j\in V \label{eq:dcost}\\
\mathrm{rcost}_{ij} &= \mathrm{rt}_{ij}\,C^r_w + \mathrm{rdist}_{ij}\,C_{rob}, & i,j\in V \label{eq:rcost}
\end{align}

\noindent Sortie cost $\gamma(s)$ in Section~\ref{sec:sorties} sums $\mathrm{dcost}$
(drone sorties, $s\in S^d$) or $\mathrm{rcost}$ (robot sorties, $s\in S^r$) over
$\mathrm{edges}(s)$.

\subsection{Derived service and trip-use indicators}

\begin{align}
\sigma^t_i &= \sum_{v\in T} \mathrm{vis}_{v,i}, & i\in C \label{eq:truck-service}\\
\sigma^d_j &= \sum_{d\in D}\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{\substack{s\in S^d\\ j\,\in\,\sigma(s)}} y_{d,q,s}, & j\in C \label{eq:drone-service}\\
\sigma^r_j &= \sum_{r\in R}\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{\substack{s\in S^r\\ j\,\in\,\sigma(s)}} z_{r,q,s}, & j\in C \label{eq:robot-service}\\
\mathrm{used}^d_{d,q} &= \sum_{s\in S^d} y_{d,q,s}\ \le 1, & d\in D,\ q\in Q \label{eq:used-drone-trip}\\
\mathrm{used}^r_{r,q} &= \sum_{s\in S^r} z_{r,q,s}\ \le 1, & r\in R,\ q\in Q \label{eq:used-robot-trip}
\end{align}

\noindent $\mathrm{used}^d_{d,q}$ ($\mathrm{used}^r_{r,q}$) is a linear
expression, not a new variable: it is $1$ iff drone $d$ (robot $r$) flies a
$q$-th trip.

\section{Objective Function}
\label{sec:objective}

\subsection{Variable (distance/time) cost}
\begin{align}
\mathrm{VarCost}^t &= \sum_{v\in T}\sum_{(i,j)\in A} \mathrm{tcost}_{ij}\,x_{v,i,j} \label{eq:varcost-truck}\\
\mathrm{VarCost}^d &= \sum_{d\in D}\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^d} \gamma(s)\,y_{d,q,s} \label{eq:varcost-drone}\\
\mathrm{VarCost}^r &= \sum_{r\in R}\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^r} \gamma(s)\,z_{r,q,s} \label{eq:varcost-robot}\\
\mathrm{VarCost} &= \mathrm{VarCost}^t + \mathrm{VarCost}^d + \mathrm{VarCost}^r \label{eq:varcost-total}
\end{align}

\subsection{Fixed activation cost}
\begin{align}
\mathrm{FixCost}^t &= f_t \sum_{v\in T} \eta_v \label{eq:fixcost-truck}\\
\mathrm{FixCost}^d &= f_d \sum_{d\in D} \eta^d_d \label{eq:fixcost-drone}\\
\mathrm{FixCost}^r &= f_r \sum_{r\in R} \eta^r_r \label{eq:fixcost-robot}\\
\mathrm{FixCost} &= \mathrm{FixCost}^t + \mathrm{FixCost}^d + \mathrm{FixCost}^r \label{eq:fixcost-total}
\end{align}
where $\eta^d_d \ge y_{d,q,s}$ for all $q,s$ and
$\eta^d_d \le \sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^d} y_{d,q,s}$
(robot analogues for $\eta^r_r$), so $\eta^d_d = 1$ iff drone $d$ flies at
least one trip.

\noindent Fixed platform cost is per \emph{used platform} (Malik et al.,
VRP-DR, Table~3): a platform flying several trips still activates once. With
$Q_{\max}=1$ this coincides with the legacy per-sortie charge.

\subsection{Operating cost, makespan blend, and penalties}
\begin{align}
\mathrm{OpCost} &= \mathrm{VarCost} + \mathrm{FixCost} \label{eq:opcost}\\
\mathrm{Pen}_Q &= \lambda_Q \sum_{v\in T} \varepsilon^Q_v \label{eq:pen-cap}\\
\mathrm{Pen}_E &= \lambda^d_E \sum_{d,q} \varepsilon^{Ed}_{d,q}
               + \lambda^r_E \sum_{r,q} \varepsilon^{Er}_{r,q} \label{eq:pen-end}\\
\mathrm{Pen}_T &= \lambda_T\, \rho^* \label{eq:pen-route}\\
\mathrm{Pen}_W &= \lambda_W \left(\sum_{i\in C}\ell^t_i + \sum_{i\in C}\ell^d_i + \sum_{i\in C}\ell^r_i\right) \label{eq:pen-tw}\\
\mathrm{TotalPen} &= \mathrm{Pen}_Q + \mathrm{Pen}_E + \mathrm{Pen}_T + \mathrm{Pen}_W \label{eq:totalpen}
\end{align}

\subsection{Objective}
\begin{align}
\min\quad P = \alpha\cdot\mathrm{OpCost} + (1-\alpha)\,\Gamma + \mathrm{TotalPen} \label{eq:objective}
\end{align}

\noindent This is the original TDRP formulation's penalized objective
(Eq.~12: operating cost plus $\lambda_Q, \lambda^d_E, \lambda^r_E, \lambda_T,
\lambda_W$ violation prices) with the VRP-DR cost--makespan scalarization
$\alpha\cdot\mathrm{OpCost} + (1-\alpha)\,\Gamma$ in place of pure operating
cost. Penalties sit \emph{outside} the $\alpha$ blend: they are already in cost
units, so feasibility pricing must not vanish as $\alpha\to 0$.
With $\alpha = 1$, $\Gamma$ dropped, and
$\lambda_Q,\lambda^d_E,\lambda^r_E\to\infty$, \eqref{eq:objective} reproduces
the previous strict model; the code exposes this via a
\texttt{relaxed\_objective} flag so strict-optimal and relaxed-optimal results
remain distinguishable in provenance.

\section{Constraints}
\label{sec:constraints}

\subsection{Customer service (assignment)}
\begin{align}
\sigma^t_i + \sigma^d_i + \sigma^r_i = 1, \qquad i\in C \label{eq:service}
\end{align}
\noindent Every customer is served exactly once, by exactly one of truck,
drone, or robot. Hard in both friend formulations.

\subsection{Relaxed truck capacity}
\label{sec:rel-cap}
\begin{align}
\varepsilon^Q_v &\ge \sum_{i\in C} q_i\, \mathrm{vis}_{v,i} - Q_t, & v\in T \label{eq:cap-truck-soft}\\
\varepsilon^Q_v &\ge 0, & v\in T \label{eq:cap-truck-nonneg}
\end{align}
\noindent The hard truck-capacity constraint is replaced by the excess variable
$\varepsilon^Q_v$ priced at $\lambda_Q$ in \eqref{eq:pen-cap} --- the
$\lambda_Q$ term of the original formulation's Eq.~12.

\subsection{Drone/robot per-sortie payload (hard)}
\begin{align}
q(s)\, y_{d,q,s} &\le Q_d, & d\in D,\ q\in Q,\ s\in S^d \label{eq:cap-drone}\\
q(s)\, z_{r,q,s} &\le Q_r, & r\in R,\ q\in Q,\ s\in S^r \label{eq:cap-robot}
\end{align}
\noindent Stays hard: the original formulation's Eq.~3 states these as hard
conditionals ($q_j \le Q_d$ if served by drone), not penalized terms, and
generation already enforces them. Relaxing them buys nothing.

\subsection{Relaxed energy/endurance}
\label{sec:rel-end}
For every $d\in D$, $q\in Q$ (robot analogues for $r\in R$):
\begin{align}
\varepsilon^{Ed}_{d,q} &\ge \sum_{s\in S^d} e(s)\,y_{d,q,s} - b^d_{d,q}, \label{eq:end-drone-soft}\\
\varepsilon^{Ed}_{d,q} &\ge 0 \label{eq:end-drone-nonneg}
\end{align}
\noindent The hard distance-endurance constraints are replaced by per-trip
battery-excess variables priced at $\lambda^d_E, \lambda^r_E$ in
\eqref{eq:pen-end} --- the $\lambda^d_E, \lambda^r_E$ terms of Eq.~12, now in
energy units and charged against the trip's battery level $b^d_{d,q}$
(Section~\ref{sec:charging}), not the nameplate capacity. Energy binds
for the drone ($B_d$ sized to the 40~km range); for the robot the battery is
$\sim$5$\times$ oversized for 12~km sorties, so $\varepsilon^{Er}$ is
structurally near-zero at our scale. With $Q_{\max}=1$,
$b^d_{d,1} = B_d$ and \eqref{eq:end-drone-soft} collapses to the single-trip
priced excess over nameplate capacity.

\subsection{Drone sortie--truck assignment and synchronization}
\label{sec:sync}
For every $d\in D$, $q\in Q$, $s\in S^d$:
\begin{align}
\sum_{v\in T} h^L_{v,d,q,s} &= y_{d,q,s} \label{eq:drone-launch-assign}\\
\sum_{v\in T} h^R_{v,d,q,s} &= y_{d,q,s} \label{eq:drone-recover-assign}
\end{align}
and for every $v\in T$:
\begin{align}
h^L_{v,d,q,s} &\le
\begin{cases}
\eta_v, & \iota(s) = 0\\
\mathrm{vis}_{v,\iota(s)}, & \iota(s)\in C\\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \label{eq:drone-launch-sync}\\
h^R_{v,d,q,s} &\le
\begin{cases}
\eta_v, & \kappa(s) = n+1\\
\mathrm{vis}_{v,\kappa(s)}, & \kappa(s)\in C\\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \label{eq:drone-recover-sync}
\end{align}
\noindent A selected trip must have exactly one launch truck and one recovery
truck, and that truck must physically be at the launch/recovery node. Launch
and recovery trucks are independent (flexible docking); with $q$ indexed, trip
$q$ may recover to truck $w_1$ at $k_1$ while trip $q+1$ launches from truck
$w_2$ at $i_2$. The robot analogues use $g^L, g^R, z$.

\subsection{Multi-trip sequencing}
\label{sec:multitrip}
For every $d\in D$ and $q \in \{1,\dots,Q_{\max}-1\}$:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{used}^d_{d,q+1} &\le \mathrm{used}^d_{d,q} \label{eq:trip-order-drone}\\
\mathrm{lt}^d_{d,q+1} &\ge \mathrm{rt}^d_{d,q} + \tau_{\mathrm{turn}}
  - M\,\big(2 - \mathrm{used}^d_{d,q} - \mathrm{used}^d_{d,q+1}\big) \label{eq:trip-seq-drone}
\end{align}
and the robot analogues for $r\in R$. Trip $q+1$ may only launch after trip
$q$ has been recovered (plus turnaround), whenever both trips are flown.
Per-trip launch/recovery times link to trucks and sorties in
Section~\ref{sec:sortie-time}:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{lt}^d_{d,q} &\ge a^t_{v,\iota(s)} + \mathrm{dt}_{\iota(s),\sigma_1(s)}
  - M\,(1-h^L_{v,d,q,s}), & \forall\,v,\,(d,q,s) \label{eq:trip-launch-time}\\
\mathrm{rt}^d_{d,q} &\ge a^d_{\sigma_m(s)} + \theta_{\sigma_m(s)}
  + \mathrm{dt}_{\sigma_m(s),\kappa(s)} - M\,(1-y_{d,q,s}), & \forall\,(d,q,s) \label{eq:trip-recover-time}
\end{align}
\noindent \eqref{eq:trip-order-drone} canonically orders trips within a
platform (eliminating trip-permutation symmetry). The per-customer arrival
chain $a^d_i$ is reused because each customer is served exactly once, so
$a^d_{\sigma_m(s)}$ is well-defined regardless of which trip serves it.

\subsection{Battery and en-route charging}
\label{sec:charging}
For every $d\in D$ and $q \in \{1,\dots,Q_{\max}-1\}$ (robot analogues for
$r\in R$):
\begin{align}
b^d_{d,1} &= B_d \label{eq:batt-start}\\
b^d_{d,q+1} &= b^d_{d,q} - \sum_{s\in S^d} e(s)\,y_{d,q,s} + c^d_{d,q} \label{eq:batt-inventory}\\
0 \le b^d_{d,q} &\le B_d \label{eq:batt-bounds}\\
c^d_{d,q} &\le r^c_d\,\big(\mathrm{lt}^d_{d,q+1} - \mathrm{rt}^d_{d,q}\big) \label{eq:charge-rate}\\
c^d_{d,q} &\le B_d\,\mathrm{used}^d_{d,q+1} \label{eq:charge-purpose}
\end{align}
\noindent \eqref{eq:batt-start}--\eqref{eq:batt-bounds} are the battery
inventory (cf.\ VRP-DR Eqs.~30--33, 41--42); the lower bound enforces ``cannot
spend uncharged energy'' through \eqref{eq:batt-inventory}.
\eqref{eq:charge-rate} caps charged energy by the charging rate times the
\emph{aboard time} $\mathrm{lt}^d_{d,q+1} - \mathrm{rt}^d_{d,q}$ --- the
interval between recovery and next launch, during which the platform is by
construction aboard the truck (cf.\ VRP-DR Eqs.~38--40; their ``charging time
$\le$ truck leg time'' is automatic here because aboard time is
truck-synchronized). \eqref{eq:charge-purpose} forbids charging into the void.
With $Q_{\max}=1$, \eqref{eq:batt-start}--\eqref{eq:charge-purpose} are vacuous
and \eqref{eq:end-drone-soft} prices the single trip's excess over the
nameplate battery. \emph{Modeling note:} under flexible docking, if
trip $q+1$ launches from a different truck than trip $q$'s recovery truck, the
platform must transfer trucks mid-gap; charging should then accrue only on the
truck-carried sub-intervals. The code requires same-truck consecutive trips
unless a truck--truck rendezvous is modeled.

\subsection{Truck timing with ready times (cyclic-ready)}
\label{sec:truck-time}
\begin{align}
a^t_{v,0} &= 0, & v\in T \label{eq:truck-start-time}\\
\mathrm{dep}^t_{v,i} &\ge a^t_{v,i} + \theta_i, & v\in T,\ i\in C \label{eq:truck-ready-cust}\\
\mathrm{dep}^t_{v,i} &\ge a^t_{v,i}, & v\in T,\ i\in P \label{eq:truck-ready-depot}\\
a^t_{v,j} &\ge \mathrm{dep}^t_{v,i} + \mathrm{tt}_{ij} - M\,(1-x_{v,i,j}), & v\in T,\ (i,j)\in A \label{eq:truck-time-propagation}\\
a^t_{v,i} &\ge e_i - M\,(1-\mathrm{vis}_{v,i}), & v\in T,\ i\in C \label{eq:truck-open}\\
\ell^t_i &\ge a^t_{v,i} - \bar{e}_i - M\,(1-\mathrm{vis}_{v,i}), & v\in T,\ i\in C \label{eq:truck-late}
\end{align}
\noindent $\mathrm{dep}^t_{v,i}$ is the truck's \emph{ready (departure) time} at
node $i$: arrival plus service, plus any waiting for returning platforms.
Separating arrival $a^t_{v,i}$ from availability $\mathrm{dep}^t_{v,i}$ is what
makes \emph{cyclic} sorties feasible --- with launch node $=$ recovery node,
the old form $a^t_{v,i} \ge a^t_{v,i} + \text{duration}$ was infeasible for any
positive-duration sortie; now the truck simply waits,
$\mathrm{dep}^t_{v,i} \ge a^t_{v,i} + \text{duration}$. For acyclic sorties the
behavior is unchanged (waiting becomes explicit rather than implicit).

\subsection{Drone and robot time windows}
\label{sec:drone-robot-tw}
\begin{align}
a^d_0 &= 0, \qquad a^r_0 = 0 \label{eq:start-time-platforms}\\
a^d_i &\ge e_i\, \sigma^d_i, & i\in C \label{eq:open-drone}\\
a^r_i &\ge e_i\, \sigma^r_i, & i\in C \label{eq:open-robot}\\
\ell^d_i &\ge a^d_i - \bar{e}_i, & i\in C \label{eq:late-drone-def}\\
\ell^r_i &\ge a^r_i - \bar{e}_i, & i\in C \label{eq:late-robot-def}\\
a^d_i &\le \bar{e}_i + \ell^d_i + M\,(1-\sigma^d_i), & i\in C \label{eq:tw-drone}\\
a^r_i &\le \bar{e}_i + \ell^r_i + M\,(1-\sigma^r_i), & i\in C \label{eq:tw-robot}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Reporting note.} For a customer $i$ with $\sigma^d_i=0$,
\eqref{eq:open-drone} reduces to $a^d_i\ge 0$ and, since $\lambda_W$ drives
$\ell^d_i\to 0$, \eqref{eq:tw-drone} reduces to $a^d_i\le \bar{e}_i$. The variable
$a^d_i$ then has no effect on the objective, so the solver may set it to either
bound of $[0,\bar{e}_i]$ with no physical meaning. The Arrival Times sheet flags such
rows as \texttt{not\_served\_value\_arbitrary} (same for $a^r_i$).

\subsection{Ordered sortie timing}
\label{sec:sortie-time}
For $d\in D$, $q\in Q$, $s\in S^d$ with sequence $\sigma(s)=(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_m)$,
launch node $\iota(s)$, recovery node $\kappa(s)$, and for every $v\in T$:
\begin{align}
a^d_{\sigma_1} &\ge a^t_{v,\iota(s)} + \mathrm{dt}_{\iota(s),\sigma_1} - M\,(1-h^L_{v,d,q,s}) \label{eq:sortie-drone-launch}\\
\mathrm{dep}^t_{v,\kappa(s)} &\ge a^d_{\sigma_m} + \theta_{\sigma_m} + \mathrm{dt}_{\sigma_m,\kappa(s)} - M\,(1-h^R_{v,d,q,s}) \label{eq:sortie-drone-recover}
\end{align}
and for every consecutive pair $(\sigma_l,\sigma_{l+1})$ in the sequence:
\begin{align}
a^d_{\sigma_{l+1}} &\ge a^d_{\sigma_l} + \theta_{\sigma_l} + \mathrm{dt}_{\sigma_l,\sigma_{l+1}} - M\,(1-y_{d,q,s}) \label{eq:sortie-drone-seq}
\end{align}
The robot analogues replace $a^d, h^L, h^R, y, \mathrm{dt}$ with $a^r, g^L, g^R, z,
\mathrm{rt}$:
\begin{align}
a^r_{\sigma_1} &\ge a^t_{v,\iota(s)} + \mathrm{rt}_{\iota(s),\sigma_1} - M\,(1-g^L_{v,r,q,s}) \label{eq:sortie-robot-launch}\\
\mathrm{dep}^t_{v,\kappa(s)} &\ge a^r_{\sigma_m} + \theta_{\sigma_m} + \mathrm{rt}_{\sigma_m,\kappa(s)} - M\,(1-g^R_{v,r,q,s}) \label{eq:sortie-robot-recover}\\
a^r_{\sigma_{l+1}} &\ge a^r_{\sigma_l} + \theta_{\sigma_l} + \mathrm{rt}_{\sigma_l,\sigma_{l+1}} - M\,(1-z_{r,q,s}) \label{eq:sortie-robot-seq}
\end{align}
\noindent Recovery constrains the truck's \emph{ready time}
$\mathrm{dep}^t_{v,\kappa(s)}$, not its arrival: the recovery truck waits for
the platform before departing. A late platform therefore propagates into that
truck's $a^t_{v,n+1}$ and hence into $\rho^t_v$, $\rho^*$, and $\Gamma$.

\subsection{Truck routing completeness}
\label{sec:routing}
For every $v\in T$:
\begin{align}
\sum_{j:(0,j)\in A} x_{v,0,j} &= \eta_v \label{eq:depot-depart}\\
\sum_{i:(i,n+1)\in A} x_{v,i,n+1} &= \eta_v \label{eq:depot-return}
\end{align}
For every $v\in T$ and $\mathrm{node}\in C$ (flow conservation):
\begin{align}
\sum_{i:(i,\mathrm{node})\in A} x_{v,i,\mathrm{node}} = \sum_{j:(\mathrm{node},j)\in A} x_{v,\mathrm{node},j} \label{eq:flow}
\end{align}
For every $v\in T,\ i\in C$:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{vis}_{v,i} &= \sum_{h:(h,i)\in A} x_{v,h,i} \label{eq:visit-def}
\end{align}
For every $v\in T$:
\begin{align}
\sum_{i\in C} \mathrm{vis}_{v,i} &\le n\,\eta_v \label{eq:used-upper}\\
\eta_v &\le \sum_{i\in C} \mathrm{vis}_{v,i} \label{eq:used-lower}\\
x_{v,i,j} &\le \eta_v, & (i,j)\in A \label{eq:arc-activation}
\end{align}
\noindent These constraints are not written explicitly in either friend
formulation, which defines $x_{v,i,j}$ but not full route-continuity; they are
the standard constraints needed for $x$ to form valid physical truck routes.

\subsection{Route-duration constraints}
For every $v\in T$:
\begin{align}
\rho^t_v &\ge a^t_{v,n+1} - T_{\max} - M\,(1-\eta_v) \label{eq:route-late-def}\\
a^t_{v,n+1} &\le T_{\max} + \rho^t_v + M\,(1-\eta_v) \label{eq:route-late-bound}\\
\rho^t_v &\le M\,\eta_v \label{eq:route-late-activation}\\
\rho^t_v &\le \rho^* + M\,(1-\eta_v) \label{eq:route-late-global-bound}
\end{align}
\noindent Because every truck departs at time $0$ (\eqref{eq:truck-start-time}),
$a^t_{v,n+1}$ \emph{is} that truck's route duration. $\rho^*$ is forced, by
\eqref{eq:route-late-activation}--\eqref{eq:route-late-global-bound} together
with the objective minimizing $\lambda_T\rho^*$, to equal $\max_{v\in
T,\,\eta_v=1} \rho^t_v$, i.e.\ the worst per-truck route-duration excess over
$T_{\max}$.

\paragraph{Relation to drone/robot routes.} The original PDF writes the
route-duration condition for $m\in\{t,d,r\}$ (trucks, drones, robots
separately). This implementation enforces it only through the truck end-depot
arrival $a^t_{v,n+1}$. A drone/robot finishing a sortie late forces its recovery
truck to wait (via \eqref{eq:sortie-drone-recover}/\eqref{eq:sortie-robot-recover}),
which propagates into that truck's $a^t_{v,n+1}$ and hence into $\rho^t_v$,
$\rho^*$, and $\Gamma$. So drone/robot lateness is captured indirectly through
the truck term, not as a separate $a^d_{n+1}, a^r_{n+1}$ penalty. See
Section~\ref{sec:deviations}.

\subsection{MTZ subtour elimination}
For every $v\in T$:
\begin{align}
u_{v,i} &\le n\,\mathrm{vis}_{v,i}, & i\in C \label{eq:mtz-upper}\\
u_{v,i} &\ge \mathrm{vis}_{v,i}, & i\in C \label{eq:mtz-lower}\\
u_{v,i} - u_{v,j} + n\,x_{v,i,j} &\le n-1, & i\ne j\in C,\ (i,j)\in A \label{eq:mtz}
\end{align}

\subsection{Truck--platform pairing limits}
For every $v\in T$:
\begin{align}
\sum_{d\in D} \xi_{v,d} &\le \mu^{trk}_d\,\eta_v \label{eq:drone-truck-cap}\\
\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^d}\big(h^L_{v,d,q,s} + h^R_{v,d,q,s}\big) &\le 2Q_{\max}\,\xi_{v,d}, & d\in D \label{eq:drone-truck-upper}\\
\xi_{v,d} &\le \sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^d}\big(h^L_{v,d,q,s} + h^R_{v,d,q,s}\big), & d\in D \label{eq:drone-truck-lower}\\[4pt]
\sum_{r\in R} \zeta_{v,r} &\le \mu^{trk}_r\,\eta_v \label{eq:robot-truck-cap}\\
\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^r}\big(g^L_{v,r,q,s} + g^R_{v,r,q,s}\big) &\le 2Q_{\max}\,\zeta_{v,r}, & r\in R \label{eq:robot-truck-upper}\\
\zeta_{v,r} &\le \sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^r}\big(g^L_{v,r,q,s} + g^R_{v,r,q,s}\big), & r\in R \label{eq:robot-truck-lower}
\end{align}
\noindent The upper-bound coefficient is $2Q_{\max}$, not $2$: with up to
$Q_{\max}$ trips per platform, a platform may launch and recover up to
$Q_{\max}$ times each with one truck. $\xi_{v,d}$ ($\zeta_{v,r}$) still counts
\emph{distinct} platforms per truck, so \eqref{eq:drone-truck-cap} semantics
are unchanged.

\subsection{Launch/recovery node capacity limits}
For every $v\in T$ and every node $w\in V$ that is a launch node of some
$s\in S^d$:
\begin{align}
\sum_{d\in D}\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{\substack{s\in S^d\\ \iota(s)=w}} h^L_{v,d,q,s} \le
\begin{cases}
\mu^{dep}_d\,\eta_v, & w = 0\\
\mu^{trk}_d\,\mathrm{vis}_{v,w}, & w\ne 0
\end{cases} \label{eq:drone-launch-limit}
\end{align}
and for every recovery node $w\in V$ that is a recovery node of some $s\in S^d$:
\begin{align}
\sum_{d\in D}\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{\substack{s\in S^r\\ \kappa(s)=w}} h^R_{v,d,q,s} \le
\begin{cases}
\mu^{trk}_d\,\eta_v, & w = n+1\\
\mu^{trk}_d\,\mathrm{vis}_{v,w}, & w\in C
\end{cases} \label{eq:drone-recovery-limit}
\end{align}
\noindent with the symmetric pair for robots using $g^L, g^R, \mu^{dep}_r,
\mu^{trk}_r$. A platform may now launch twice from one node on different
trips; the sums count trips correctly.

\subsection{Symmetry breaking}
\begin{align}
\mathrm{used}^d_{d,q+1} &\le \mathrm{used}^d_{d,q}, & d\in D,\ q<Q_{\max} \label{eq:sym-trip-drone}\\
\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^d} y_{d+1,q,s} &\le \sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^d} y_{d,q,s}, & d\in\{1,\dots,|D|-1\} \label{eq:sym-drone}\\
\sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^r} z_{r+1,q,s} &\le \sum_{q\in Q}\sum_{s\in S^r} z_{r,q,s}, & r\in\{1,\dots,|R|-1\} \label{eq:sym-robot}
\end{align}
\noindent \eqref{eq:sym-trip-drone} (same as \eqref{eq:trip-order-drone})
canonically orders trips within a platform; \eqref{eq:sym-drone} generalizes
the old platform ordering to total trip counts. Valid: platforms are identical
and trips are canonically ordered within each platform, so sorting platforms
by trip count descending loses nothing.

\subsection{Makespan}
\begin{align}
\Gamma &\ge a^t_{v,n+1} - M\,(1-\eta_v), & v\in T \label{eq:makespan-truck}\\
\Gamma &\ge \mathrm{rt}^d_{d,q} - M\,(1-\mathrm{used}^d_{d,q}), & d\in D,\ q\in Q \label{eq:makespan-drone}\\
\Gamma &\ge \mathrm{rt}^r_{r,q} - M\,(1-\mathrm{used}^r_{r,q}), & r\in R,\ q\in Q \label{eq:makespan-robot}\\
\Gamma &\ge 0 \label{eq:makespan-nonneg}
\end{align}
\noindent $\Gamma$ is the latest completion time over all used trucks and all
flown platform trips. Unlike the previous model (makespan reported only), it
is a decision variable entering the objective through \eqref{eq:objective}.

\section{Lossless Fleet Trimming}
\label{sec:trimming}

Before $D$ and $R$ are fixed, the raw fleet sizes from \texttt{parameters.json}
(\texttt{NUM\_DRONES}, \texttt{NUM\_ROBOTS}) are trimmed to
\begin{align}
|D| &= \max\big(1,\ \min(\mu^{trk}_d\cdot|T|,\ n)\big) \label{eq:trim-drone}\\
|R| &= \max\big(1,\ \min(\mu^{trk}_r\cdot|T|,\ n)\big) \label{eq:trim-robot}
\end{align}
\noindent The lossless argument is unchanged under multi-trip:
\eqref{eq:drone-truck-cap} caps distinct usable drones at
$\mu^{trk}_d\cdot|T|$, and \eqref{eq:service} caps used platforms at $n$ (each
used platform serves at least one customer across its trips; each customer is
served once), so any platform beyond $|D|$ (or $|R|$) is provably unused in
every feasible solution. Multi-trip changes the \emph{economics} (fewer
platforms needed per customer), not the validity of the trim.

\section{Time-Dependent Travel Times}
\label{sec:td}

The original PDF formulation assumes time-dependent travel times $t_{ij}(\tau)$.
The MILP above is static: every timing constraint uses the free-flow matrix
$\mathrm{tt}_{ij}$. The codebase ships a time-dependent truck layer for
a~posteriori evaluation of solutions under $t_{ij}(\tau)$ and for time-dependent
heuristic route evaluation (\texttt{scripts/time\_dependent.py},
\texttt{scripts/add\_td\_matrices.py}).

\paragraph{Method.} The working day $[0,H]$ is split into $P$ equal periods.
Each period $p$ carries a speed factor $f_p\in(0,1]$ relative to free-flow speed.
The travel time for a departure at the start of period $p$ is computed by
\emph{integrating speeds} --- the classical construction of Ichoua, Gendreau \&
Semet (EJOR 144(3), 2003): find the smallest $T$ with
$\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_0+T} f(\tau)\,d\tau = \mathrm{tt}_{ij}$, where $\tau_0$ is
the departure time and $f(\cdot)$ is the stepwise speed profile (repeated
cyclically past the horizon). Because speeds, not travel times, are the
primitive, the FIFO property holds by construction: departing later can never
arrive earlier. The generator asserts \texttt{verify\_fifo} on every build.

\paragraph{Profiles.} Speed factors are defined as resolution-independent
piecewise segments over day fractions and discretized at period midpoints, so
any $P$ can be used:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{two\_peak}: two-peak urban day, maximum slowdown $2{:}1$, within
Figliozzi's (TRE 48(3), 2012) $2.5{:}1$ empirical bound;
\item \texttt{mild}: small-city congestion (e.g.\ DeKalb, IL), slowest factor $0.80$;
\item \texttt{flat}: all factors $1.0$, the static control.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope and files.} Only the truck layer is time-dependent. Robot
(pedestrian) congestion data does not exist publicly and drone airspace is
uncongested, so $\mathrm{dt}_{ij}$ and $\mathrm{rt}_{ij}$ stay static; this is
recorded in the instance provenance. For each profile the generator writes
\texttt{truck\_time\_matrix\_\{profile\}\_p\{p\}.csv}, $p=0,\dots,P-1$, with entry
$[p][i,j]=\mathrm{tt}^p_{ij}$ in hours, and merges provenance (period bounds,
speed factors, citations, \texttt{td\_fifo\_verified}) into
\texttt{parameters.json}. Flags: \texttt{--time-periods} $P$,
\texttt{--td-profile}; existing instances are retrofitted with
\texttt{scripts/add\_td\_matrices.py}.

\paragraph{Status in this model.} The MILP of
Sections~\ref{sec:sets}--\ref{sec:trimming} optimizes the static problem. The TD
matrices are not decision-model inputs; they evaluate fixed routes under
$t_{ij}(\tau)$ (e.g.\ re-timing a proven-optimal static route through peak
periods) and will drive time-dependent heuristic search, with the exact static
optimum as the baseline for optimality-gap measurement. Optimizing directly
under time dependence would require a period-expanded MILP and is deliberately
out of scope.

\section{Deviations from the Friend Formulations}
\label{sec:deviations}

This model now \emph{follows} the two friend formulations rather than
deviating from them. Remaining differences:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Static optimization with time-dependent evaluation.} Both friend
formulations assume $t_{ij}(\tau)$ throughout; this MILP optimizes static
$\mathrm{tt}_{ij}$, while Section~\ref{sec:td} provides FIFO-safe
time-dependent matrices for evaluating solutions and heuristics under
$t_{ij}(\tau)$.
\item \textbf{Route-duration penalty is truck-indexed only}, as discussed after
\eqref{eq:route-late-global-bound}, rather than summed separately over
$m\in\{t,d,r\}$ as in the original PDF's Eq.~8/Eq.~12. Drone/robot lateness
still affects $\rho^*$ and $\Gamma$ indirectly through synchronization.
\item \textbf{Energy replaces distance as the endurance primitive.}
VRP-DR caps drone distance per sortie and models robot energy quadratically;
here both platforms use the linear Wh/km energy $e(s)$ precomputed at
generation, and the $\lambda_E$ penalties price Wh of battery excess. For our
hardware the drone battery is sized exactly to its range (energy binds); the
robot battery is $\sim$5$\times$ oversized, so robot charging is structurally
minor at this scale.
\item \textbf{Operational routing constraints}
(\eqref{eq:depot-depart}--\eqref{eq:mtz}) are added because the friend
formulations define $x_{v,i,j}$ but do not write full route-continuity
constraints; without them $x$ does not necessarily form a connected truck
route from depot to depot.
\item \textbf{Strict-model compatibility.} The previous strict model (hard
capacity/endurance, cost-only objective) is recovered exactly with $\alpha=1$,
$\Gamma$ dropped, $E_{\mathrm{slack}}=1$, and
$\lambda_Q,\lambda^d_E,\lambda^r_E\to\infty$; the code's
\texttt{relaxed\_objective} flag selects between them. Proven-optimal results
under the strict model (slice6: $67.1552$; slice8: $68.8452$) do \emph{not}
transfer to the relaxed model, which has a strictly larger feasible region and
must be re-solved for its own certificates.
\item \textbf{Big-M, waiting penalty, and raw cost fields}
(\texttt{big\_M}, \texttt{waiting\_penalty\_weight}, \texttt{truck\_variable\_cost},
\texttt{drone\_departure\_cost}) from the raw TDRP-TW parameters are superseded:
$M$ is computed internally (Section~\ref{sec:parameters}), platform waiting is
reported via the Sortie Timing Audit rather than penalized, and travel costs use
$C_{veh}/C_{drone}/C_{rob}$ with the fixed-cost terms \eqref{eq:fixcost-truck}--\eqref{eq:fixcost-robot}.
\end{itemize}

A full parameter-by-parameter audit is in \texttt{parameter\_usage\_audit.csv} and
the README's ``Parameter Usage Audit'' section.
